READY TO START?

Connect your first app in Claude.

We'll start with Claude's built-in Connectors Directory. You can connect a supported app without coding, installing a server, or opening a terminal.

Before you begin: Sign in to Claude. Choose an app you trust and are allowed to connect. The directory and available features may vary by plan or company settings.

New to MCP? Connecting a listed app is your easiest introduction. Building or configuring a custom MCP server is a different, more advanced task.

FOLLOW ALONG

One small action at a time.

⚙️

Open Customize

Go to Claude and sign in. In the left sidebar, click Customize, then Connectors.

You should see: a list of connectors or a way to browse available apps.

🔍

Find an app

Click the + next to Connectors to open the directory. Pick an app your organization approves.

You should see: details about the app and what its connection can do.

🔐

Read permissions, then connect

Look at which information the app can read and which actions it might take. Click Connect or Install, then sign in to that service if asked. Stop if the access seems too broad.

You should see: an approval screen and, after authorizing it, a connected app.

💬

Turn it on in your chat

Open a new Claude chat. Click the + near the message box, open Connectors, and enable your app for that chat.

You should see: your connected app among the available services.

✅

Ask a tiny test question

Begin with one read-only question. Avoid giving permission to edit, send, share, or delete anything.

You should see: a source-backed result—or an explanation if nothing can be accessed.

Alternate route: In a chat, choose + → Connectors → Manage connectors to reach the same connector directory. Labels may change as Claude evolves. Check Claude's official instructions ↗

COPY-READY EXAMPLE

A safe first request.

Once you have connected an approved document app, copy this prompt into Claude:

Using only my approved connected app, find one non-sensitive marketing document I am allowed to read. Tell me its title, source, and date. Do not edit, send, share, export, or delete anything. If you cannot access the app, tell me instead of guessing.

Did it work?

  • Did Claude use the app you expected?
  • Can you open and verify the original source?
  • Did the result respect your access permissions?
  • Did Claude avoid changing or sharing records?

How to disconnect

Return to Customize → Connectors, find the app, and review its available disconnect or permission settings. If your organization manages the connection, ask your administrator.

PICK ONE TO TRY

Three beginner-friendly connector tests.

You do not need all three. Choose one app you already use and are allowed to connect. These are examples, not guarantees that every connector is available or supports every request.

DOCUMENTS · OR ANY APPROVED FILE CONNECTOR

Google Drive

Find a campaign brief

Copy this test prompt:

Using my approved document connector, find one non-sensitive campaign brief or marketing plan I can access. Give me its exact title, location or source link, and last-updated date if available. Do not edit, download, share, or delete anything. If you cannot access it, say so clearly.

You should see: A document title and source link you can open and verify; no changes to the file.

CALENDAR · OR ANY APPROVED CALENDAR CONNECTOR

Google Calendar

Check the next marketing meeting

Copy this test prompt:

Using my approved calendar connector, find my next event with “marketing” or “campaign” in the title over the next 14 days. Show the date, time, and title only. Do not create, change, invite, or cancel anything. If there is no matching event, tell me.

You should see: One matching event (or an explicit “none found”) that agrees with your calendar.

CRM · OR ANY APPROVED CRM CONNECTOR

HubSpot CRM

Look up a sample company

Copy this test prompt:

Using my approved CRM connector, find one non-sensitive company record that I am allowed to view. Return the company name, record link or ID, and last-updated date if available. Do not change, create, export, or share any records. If this connector cannot search company records, tell me rather than guessing.

You should see: A real company record you can cross-check in the CRM, or a clear explanation that the connector cannot do it.

Permission check: Start with read-only tasks and non-sensitive information. If your app is missing, unavailable on your plan, or requires administrator approval, skip it. A built-in app connection is not necessarily a custom MCP server.

OPTIONAL — ADVANCED PATH

What if your app isn't listed?

Your company or vendor might offer a custom remote MCP server. This requires a trusted server URL and sometimes extra security settings. A normal website URL is not an MCP server address.

Beginner recommendation: You do not need to set this up to complete the guide. Only use a custom connection if your organization or a trusted vendor provides the correct endpoint and permission guidance.

For supported accounts, Claude's custom connector setup starts in Customize → Connectors → Add → Custom → Web, then asks for the server details and authentication setup. Some teams need an administrator to configure or approve it.
